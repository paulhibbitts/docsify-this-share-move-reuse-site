# Why It Matters

<small>Part 1</small>

> [!TIP]
> **Learning Objectives**
>
> - **Share:** see pages designed for readers and learners, in both starters
> - **Move:** take content between tools, or from Pressbooks or an LMS
> - **Reuse:** get any page's Markdown, for your own purposes

**Open both ways:** open content is meant to be reused and remixed ([the 5Rs](https://opencontent.org/definition/)), but content that's open for readers often stays inside the platform for its authors – harder to edit elsewhere, move or keep. What if it were open for both?

> [!NOTE]
> **The DIY answer: plain Markdown**
>
> A simple text format for web pages – `**bold**`, `## Heading`, `- list item`. It's supported almost everywhere, from GitHub and note-taking apps to Google Docs, and it's widely used by other tools, including AI. Keep every page in Markdown, on a free site you control.

**Not ready to openly license your work?** Plain Markdown still keeps it yours – it outlasts any one platform, and moves, backs up and adapts easily for years to come.

**Reuse anywhere:** you (and, with an open licence, anyone) can get the Markdown behind any page they're viewing, with its callouts, math and diagrams intact – so it opens as it is in [Docsify-This](https://docsify-this.net/) (which shows Markdown as web pages) or on GitHub.

**See the proof:** [a Pressbooks book](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/universaldesign2/) and [the same book in Open Publishing Space](https://demo.hibbittsdesign.org/variants/grav-open-publishing-quark2-pressbooks-udl/), converted with no revisions – more in [From Pressbooks or Your LMS](guide/from-pressbooks-or-your-lms.md).

**One guide, many uses:** read it at your own pace, present it like slides (← and → move between pages), embed it in an LMS, print it or get its Markdown – all from the same pages. Write once, use it many ways.
